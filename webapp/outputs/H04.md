## Enquiry

We already have a silica fiber cable from a visible or NIR measurement setup and would like to reuse it. The link length, routing and operating conditions have not yet been supplied. Can the assistant approve this concept?

## Risks and problems with the request

- **Won't work:** The existing visible/NIR silica cable (about 350-2400 nm) cannot carry MIR ATR, whose water-in-oil spectra span 4000-2000 cm-1 (2.5-5 µm); mid-IR needs CIR (1.1-6.5 µm) or PIR (3-17 µm) fibers. [S02 p.6; REF §5; S02 p.13; S01 p.9]
- **Won't work:** Placing the analyzer in another building is not documented for MIR: 100-300 m cables are described only for low-OH silica, and the PIR versus hollow-waveguide comparison is shown for 2 m lengths, so AFS must confirm whether any MIR link of the required length is feasible. [S02 p.7; S02 p.9]
- **Check first:** Approval is asked before the facts allow it: fiber type, link length, routing and operating conditions are not supplied, and method selection depends on such factors. [S01 p.5; S01 p.8]
- **Check first:** The petroleum ATR example is a reason to test MIR ATR, not proof: the slide gives no detection limit, fiber link length or process conditions. [S02 p.13]

## Methods worth testing

- **MIR ATR** (Main): Same measured quantity is shown on AFS slides (oil with 0.1-2.0 % water, 4000-2000 cm-1) and MIR ATR suits liquids with high selectivity, but needs CIR/PIR fiber. [S02 p.13; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5; S01 p.10]
- **NIR transmission/transflection** (Compare): NIR works with ordinary glass optics and remote fiber probes, moisture bands lie at 1.3-2.2 µm within the Vis-NIR silica range, and long silica cables exist, so it could reuse the existing cable. [S01 p.5; S02 p.18; S02 p.6; S02 p.7; S01 p.10]
- **Raman** (Only if needed): Raman is recorded in the visible/NIR on silica fibers and carries information complementary to MIR, but signals are weak and often masked by fluorescence of organic compounds. [S01 p.5; S01 p.11; S02 p.7]
- **Fluorescence** (Not suitable): Fluorimetry relies on secondary emission of certain substances and has low selectivity, so it is not a direct route to water content. [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## Multispectral option

**MIR ATR + NIR**: Oil is named among complex media that may need two or more methods, and MIR gives well-defined absorption peaks while NIR gives broad overtone bands and suits long remote fibers.. Probe: separate synchronised probes, unless the water is heterogeneously distributed, in which case a same-spot multispectral probe is needed (a four-method MIR/NIR/Raman/fluorescence probe is only under development). Fusion: model per method first, then a dedicated data-fusion model of synchronised spectra. Prove the gain: The fused model must predict the reference water values better than the best single-method model on the same validation data. [S01 p.7; S01 p.5; S01 p.8; S01 p.9; S01 p.15]

## Clarification questions

1. What is the cable length and routing from the sampling point in the line to the analyzer building? (Long cables are documented only for silica, and MIR fiber reach must be confirmed by AFS.) [S02 p.7; S02 p.9]
2. What are the temperature, pressure, flow and cleaning conditions at the sampling point? (Probe construction (ZrO2/diamond tip in PEEK) and method selection depend on such factors.) [S02 p.13; S01 p.5]
3. What water content range must be measured and with what accuracy? (The AFS example covers 0.1-2.0 % water only and states no detection limit.) [S02 p.13]
4. Which independent laboratory analysis can provide reference water values for the samples? (Quantitative use requires chemometric calibration models that are built and validated against reference data.) [S01 p.5]
5. Is the water dissolved, emulsified or present as separate droplets or layers? (ATR samples only a thin layer of 1-6 µm, so heterogeneity at the probe surface affects what is measured.) [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5; S01 p.8]

## First feasibility experiment

Check in the laboratory whether MIR ATR and NIR see the water content differences in the client's petroleum, before any in-line concept is approved. [S02 p.20; S05 p.1; S01 p.3; S01 p.5; S02 p.13; S02 p.9]

1. Collect client petroleum samples covering the expected water range, each with an independent laboratory water analysis.
2. Measure the samples with a ZrO2 or diamond ATR probe on CIR/PIR fiber coupled to an FTIR spectrometer.
3. Measure the same samples with an NIR transmission/transflection probe on Vis-NIR silica fiber.
4. Identify key spectral features and build and validate a chemometric model for each method against the reference values.
5. Ask AFS to confirm the feasible MIR fiber length and compare it with the client's link length.

References: Independent laboratory water-content analysis of each sample (method to be named by the client)
Go / no-go: criteria come from the client

## Sources cited

- **S02 p.6** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Fiber transmission ranges: UV-Vis silica 190–1200 nm; Vis-NIR silica 350–2400 nm; chalcogenide As-S (CIR) 1.1–6.5 µm; polycrystalline (PIR) 3–17 µm; hollow glass waveguides 2–18 µm; InF₃ 0.3–5.5 µm; ZrF₄ 0.3–4.5 µm (figure values).
- **S02 p.13** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Application: measurement of water content in petroleum. States that ATR probes with ATR tips of ZrO2 (and diamond etc.) sealed in a PEEK shaft and protected by PEEK tubing can be used in microwave furnaces. Chart: IR absorbance spectra (4000-2000 cm-1) for pure oil and for oil with 0.1%, 0.5%, 1.0% and 2.0% water, with absorbance increasing with water content in the shown range. The slide gives no detection limit, fiber link length or process conditions.
- **REF §5** (AFS_Examples_and_Reference.docx): Unit conversion: wavelength in µm = 10 000 / wavenumber in cm⁻¹ (4000 cm⁻¹ = 2.5 µm; 400 cm⁻¹ = 25 µm).
- **S02 p.7** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Robust 100–300 m cables of low-OH silica fibers enable remote industrial process control (Vis-NIR). Al/Cu-coated silica fibers offer high-temperature resistance and no polymer-jacket fluorescence for Raman and fluorescence.
- **S02 p.9** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Hollow waveguides have low NA and couple poorly to FTIR spectrometers; PIR fibers (NA > 0.3) couple much better in ATR probes. Transmission comparison shown for 2 m lengths.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): NIR penetrates up to several millimetres even into dense solids (low absorption, strong scattering), works with ordinary glass optics and standard lamps, which makes it suited to online use with remote fiber probes.
- **S02 p.18** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Band chart: fat/protein/moisture 0.85–1.1 µm; moisture/polymers/API/ethanol 1.3–1.7, 1.5–2.0, 1.7–2.2 µm; hydrocarbons 3.0–3.7 µm; ethanol/glucose/organic acids 6.0–8.0, 8.0–10.5 µm. A central analyzer with long fibers to many points is contrasted with distributed IoT sensors.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Selecting a spectral method for a specific analytical problem requires considering many factors and specialised knowledge.
- **S02 p.13** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Water in petroleum: ATR probes with ZrO₂ (or diamond) tips sealed in PEEK; example MIR absorbance spectra 4000–2000 cm⁻¹ of oil with 0.1–2.0 % water.
- **S01 Table 1 (p.6)** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Table 1 rates methods qualitatively: MIR ATR (liquid/paste/soft, depth 1–6 µm, sensitivity average, selectivity high, cost high); NIR transmission/transflection probe (liquid/colloidal, 1–10 mm); NIR diffuse reflection probe (scattering media/powder, 0.5–1 mm); Raman probe (liquid/powder/solid, 2–5 mm, selectivity high, cost high); UV/Vis probes (selectivity low); fluorimetric probe (liquid/solid, 1–5 mm, sensitivity high, selectivity low, cost low).
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Mid-IR spectra contain well-defined absorption peaks, while NIR, UV and fluorescence give broad overlapping spectra; quantitative use requires chemometric calibration or classification models that are built, validated and then applied.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Strong mid-IR absorption limits MIR to very thin layers (usually <50 µm), which is why MIR is used with ATR attachments and probes.
- **S01 p.8** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): For heterogeneous samples the different methods must see the same small area; alternating two probes makes this hard, multispectral probes solve it. Data from methods with different acquisition rates must be synchronised.
- **S02 p.20** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): AFS pathway: multi-method lab trials on the customer process, then identification of key spectral features, then a chemometric model, a customised system or IoT sensors.
- **S05 p.1** (S05_AFS_Flyer.pdf): AFS guided pathway: (1) multi-method lab test with the AFS multi-spectral fiber system, (2) selection of key spectral features by multivariate analysis and fusion, (3) tailored solution: Path A AI-driven chemometric model (spectra to real-time composition), Path B customised MSF system (tailored spectrometer set for the customer's own R&D), Path C customised spectral fiber sensors (SFS) for a specific process with remote/cloud control.
- **S01 p.3** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): For samples of incompletely known composition it is not always possible to predict which method will work; testing several optical approaches is the way to select the most suitable method or combination.
- **S01 p.9** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Mid-IR vibrational spectroscopy covers 4000–400 cm⁻¹ and is one of the most informative methods for chemical analysis.
- **S01 p.8** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): High accuracy can be achieved by using specially developed multispectral probes. One such probe, optionally supplied with the multispectral laboratory, allows for the combination of Mid-IR spectral measurements using the ATR method with fluorescence and even Raman spectra. Additional measurements are performed through an A TR zirconium oxide crystal, shaped like a truncated cone. For this purpose, an additional pair of silica fibers transmitting in the UV and visible regions is connected to the base of the crystal. Another multispectral probe is also under development, combining four spectral methods: Mid-IR, NIR, Raman, and fluorescence. The problem of simultaneous measurements also arises in the multispectral study of fast processes.
- **S01 p.10** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): ATR fiber probes analyse liquids and elastic solids at a distance from the spectrometer without sampling, using high-transmission mid-IR fibers.
- **S01 p.10** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): NIR (780–2500 nm) is the most common online fiber-probe method; its broad overlapping overtone/combination bands require chemometrics; diffuse reflectance allows measurements on scattering samples.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman needs special probes, is recorded in the visible/NIR region, and carries information complementary to MIR.
- **S01 p.11** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman signals are weak and often masked by laser scatter and, for many organic compounds, by much stronger fluorescence; special filters and probe designs are needed. The Raman shift (cm⁻¹) is the spectral axis.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fluorimetry is highly sensitive but relies on secondary emission of certain substances, mostly of biological origin; most chemicals do not fluoresce.
- **S01 p.7** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Complex media (oil, milk, fermentation media, tissue) may need two or more methods; examples: NIR+fluorescence gave more accurate biomass in yeast fermentation and distinguished intra- and extracellular metabolites; NIR+IR showed benefits for tumour boundaries; fluorescence+IR was needed to classify tissues; NIR+Raman gave the best results for pellet coating.
- **S01 p.9** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fused data blocks differ in noise and information content; normalisation alone does not fix this, so dedicated data-fusion algorithms are needed.
- **S01 p.15** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): The cited feed-frame study reports that fused NIR+Raman PLS models predicted API potency more accurately than single-method models (in that study).
