## Risks and problems with the request

- **Won't work:** The existing visible/NIR silica cable transmits only about 350–2400 nm, but MIR starts at 4000 cm-1 = 2.5 µm and the water-in-oil ATR spectra span 4000–2000 cm-1, so MIR ATR needs CIR (1.1–6.5 µm) or PIR (3–17 µm) fiber. [S02 p.6; REF §5; S01 p.9; S02 p.13]
- **Won't work:** Long 100–300 m cables are documented only for low-OH silica (Vis-NIR), while the mid-IR fiber comparison is shown only for 2 m, so AFS must confirm whether any MIR link to another building is feasible. [S02 p.7; S02 p.9]
- **Check first:** Approval is asked before the facts allow it: fiber type, link length, routing and operating conditions are not supplied, and method selection depends on such factors. [S01 p.5]
- **Check first:** The AFS water-in-petroleum slide gives no detection limit, fiber link length or process conditions, so it is a reason to test MIR ATR, not proof it works in this line. [S02 p.13]

## Methods worth testing

- **MIR ATR** (Main): AFS shows MIR ATR spectra of oil with 0.1–2.0 % water where absorbance increases with water content, and ATR suits liquids with a 1–6 µm sampling depth. [S02 p.13; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5]
- **NIR transmission/transflection** (Compare): NIR covers moisture bands (1.3–2.2 µm), works with ordinary silica optics and remote probes, and low-OH silica supports 100–300 m links, so it may fit the existing cable and distant analyzer. [S02 p.18; S01 p.5; S02 p.7; S02 p.6]
- **Raman** (Only if needed): Raman runs on silica fibers in the visible/NIR and carries information complementary to MIR, but organic samples can mask it with fluorescence. [S01 p.5; S01 p.11]
- **Fluorescence** (Not suitable): Fluorimetry relies on secondary emission of certain substances and has low selectivity, so it is not a direct route to water content. [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## First feasibility experiment

Establish in the laboratory whether MIR ATR, and alternatively NIR, sees water content differences in the client's own oil. [S05 p.1; S02 p.13; S01 p.5; S01 p.3; S02 p.9; S02 p.7]

1. Obtain client oil samples and prepare a water-addition series covering the client's expected range.
2. Record MIR ATR spectra with a ZrO2 or diamond ATR probe on CIR fiber and NIR transmission/transflection spectra on silica fiber for each sample.
3. Determine water content of every sample with the client's independent laboratory reference method.
4. Select key spectral features and build and validate chemometric models for each method on the same samples.
5. Have AFS confirm whether a MIR fiber link of the required length is feasible; otherwise evaluate NIR over low-OH silica for the remote link.

References: Independent laboratory water determination on each sample (method unknown – ask)
Go / no-go: Accuracy and range criteria must come from the client.

- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Measurement of the weak Raman effect requires special probes but is recorded in the visible and NIR spectral regions. Moreover, the spectra of vibrational transitions in Raman and mid-IR spectroscopy carry complementary qualitative and quantitative information about the sample's composition. UV-visible spectroscopy is based on electronic transitions between molecular energy levels. It has very high sensitivity, especially in the UV region (190-360 nm), but requires the use of special light sources and fiber optic probes. However, the strong overlap of spectral signals in UV spectra complicates the analysis of real samples and makes the use of chemometric algorithms a necessary component of qualitative mixture analysis methods.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Strong mid-IR absorption limits MIR to very thin layers (usually <50 µm), which is why MIR is used with ATR attachments and probes.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): NIR penetrates up to several millimetres even into dense solids (low absorption, strong scattering), works with ordinary glass optics and standard lamps, which makes it suited to online use with remote fiber probes.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman needs special probes, is recorded in the visible/NIR region, and carries information complementary to MIR.
- **S01 p.11** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman signals are weak and often masked by laser scatter and, for many organic compounds, by much stronger fluorescence; special filters and probe designs are needed. The Raman shift (cm⁻¹) is the spectral axis.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fluorimetry is highly sensitive but relies on secondary emission of certain substances, mostly of biological origin; most chemicals do not fluoresce.
- **S02 p.16** (S02_AFS_Process_Control_Slides.pdf): Combi-Fiber Systems for ATR+Fluo/ Raman spectroscopy. - **S01 p.8** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): High accuracy can be achieved by using specially developed multispectral probes. One such probe, optionally supplied with the multispectral laboratory, allows for the combination of Mid-IR spectral measurements using the ATR method with fluorescence and even Raman spectra. Additional measurements are performed through an A TR zirconium oxide crystal, shaped like a truncated cone. For this purpose, an additional pair of silica fibers transmitting in the UV and visible regions is connected to the base of the crystal. Another multispectral probe is also under development, combining four spectral methods: Mid-IR, NIR, Raman, and fluorescence. The problem of simultaneous measurements also arises in the multispectral study of fast processes.
