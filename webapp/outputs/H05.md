## Enquiry

We have a liquid process that occasionally goes off specification. We suspect contamination, but we cannot yet name the contaminant or the affected quality variable. We would like you to recommend the best spectroscopy method and exact sensor configuration immediately. What information and first experiment should come before that decision?

## Requirements and constraints

- material: a liquid process
- conditions: occasionally goes off specification
- target: we suspect contamination, but we cannot yet name the contaminant or the affected quality variable
- goal: recommend the best spectroscopy method and exact sensor configuration immediately
- goal: What information and first experiment should come before that decision?

Missing:
- Composition of the normal liquid (main components, solvent, water content)
- Identity or class of the suspected contaminant and the quality variable that goes off specification
- Measurement location in the process (pipe, vessel, line) and process conditions (temperature, pressure, flow, cleaning, hazardous area)
- Optical properties of the liquid (transparent, turbid, opaque, fluorescent)
- Available independent laboratory reference analysis and retained in-spec and off-spec samples
- Required decision (detect/alarm vs. quantify), concentration range and response time

## Risks and problems with the request

- **Check first:** An exact configuration is asked before the target is known, but choosing a method requires knowing the analytical problem and for samples of unknown composition several methods are tested first, so an immediate exact configuration would rest on invented facts. [S01 p.5; S01 p.3]
- **Check first:** The interface (ATR, transmission, transflection or reflection probe) depends on the method and the liquid's properties, which are not yet known, so probe type and path length cannot be fixed now. [S01 p.4; S01 p.16]
- **Check first:** Detecting a contaminant with broad-band methods (NIR, UV, fluorescence) needs chemometric models built and validated against independent references, so off-spec samples and a laboratory reference analysis are required before any decision. [S01 p.5]
- **Check first:** NIR may lack sensitivity below 0.1 % and most chemicals do not fluoresce, so the contaminant level and nature decide which methods can see it at all. [S01 p.16; S01 p.5]

## Methods worth testing

- **MIR ATR** (Main): Mid-IR gives well-defined absorption peaks with high selectivity for liquids, which helps reveal an unknown chemical difference between in-spec and off-spec samples. [S01 p.9; S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.10]
- **Raman** (Compare): Raman probes liquids with high selectivity and carries information complementary to MIR, but fluorescence of organic compounds can mask its weak signal. [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5; S01 p.11]
- **NIR** (Compare): NIR is the most common online fiber-probe method with long silica cables, but its broad overlapping bands need chemometrics and it may lack sensitivity below 0.1 %. [S01 p.10; S02 p.7; S01 p.16]
- **Fluorescence / UV-Vis** (Only if needed): Both are highly sensitive but have low selectivity and fluorescence only works if the contaminant or liquid contains fluorescing substances. [S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.5]

## Multispectral option

**MIR ATR + Raman**: Raman carries information complementary to MIR, and combining heterogeneous information from two methods is the basis of the multispectral synergy effect.. Probe: Separate time-synchronised probes if the liquid is homogeneous at the measuring point; a combined multispectral probe if it is heterogeneous or changes rapidly.. Fusion: Low-level fusion of synchronised spectra with dedicated data-fusion algorithms, compared against a model per method.. Prove the gain: Build single-method and fused models on the same validation samples and accept the combination only if it beats the best single method. [S01 p.5; S01 p.7; S01 p.8; S01 p.14; S01 p.9; S01 p.14–15; S01 p.15]

## Clarification questions

1. What is the normal composition of the liquid and is it transparent, turbid or opaque? (The liquid's composition and optical density decide the method and probe interface (ATR, transmission or reflection).) [S01 p.4; S01 p.16]
2. Which quality variable goes off specification, and what candidate contaminants or sources do you suspect? (Selecting a spectral method requires knowing the analytical problem.) [S01 p.5]
3. Can you provide retained in-spec and off-spec samples together with independent laboratory analyses of them? (Several methods must be compared on real samples and chemometric models need independent reference values to be built and validated.) [S01 p.3; S01 p.5]
4. Where would the probe be installed and what are the temperature, pressure, flow, cleaning and hazardous-area conditions there? (Fiber probes allow remote in-line monitoring, but probe and fiber choice depend on the installation conditions.) [S02 p.3; S02 p.7]
5. Do you need an alarm when the liquid deviates, or a quantitative value, and how fast must it respond? (The task decides the method and technique and whether a classification or quantitative model is built.) [S01 p.4; S01 p.5]

## First feasibility experiment

Find out which optical method sees a spectral difference between retained in-spec and off-spec liquid samples, before selecting any in-line configuration. [S02 p.3; S05 p.1; S02 p.20; S01 p.3]

1. Collect retained in-spec and off-spec liquid samples and have them analysed by an independent laboratory reference method chosen by the client.
2. Measure all samples with the AFS multi-spectral fiber system using MIR ATR, Raman, NIR and fluorescence/UV-Vis probes.
3. Compare the spectra by multivariate analysis to identify which method and which spectral regions separate in-spec from off-spec samples.
4. Use the identified key spectral features to propose the method, probe interface, fiber and measurement point for a first in-line trial.

References: Independent laboratory analysis of each sample (method to be named by the client); Client's own in-spec/off-spec classification of each sample
Go / no-go: Criteria come from the client (required detection level and separation of off-spec samples).

## AFS pathway

1. Lab test: MIR ATR (main) · Raman (compare) · NIR (compare) · Fluorescence / UV-Vis (only if needed)
2. Key spectral features: Not yet known; to be identified by multivariate analysis of in-spec versus off-spec spectra.
3. Path not_yet: Path selection follows the multi-method lab test and key-feature selection, which have not been done. [S05 p.1; S02 p.20]

## Assumptions

- The process liquid can be sampled and retained in-spec and off-spec samples can be supplied for laboratory testing.
- The contaminant is chemical in nature and may produce a spectral difference; this is unverified.
