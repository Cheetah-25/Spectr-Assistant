We have a liquid process that occasionally goes off specification. We suspect contamination, but we cannot yet name the contaminant or the affected quality variable. ## Risks and problems with the request

- **Check first:** An exact method and sensor configuration is requested before the contaminant or quality variable is known; choosing a method requires knowing the analytical problem, and for samples of unknown composition several methods must be tested first, so an immediate configuration would rest on invented facts. [S01 p.5; S01 p.3]
- **Check first:** Each method needs its own interface (ATR, transmission, transflection, reflection), and the task decides method and technique, so probe type and installation cannot be specified until the liquid's properties and the target are known. [S01 p.4; S01 p.16]

## Methods worth testing

- **MIR ATR** (Main): Mid-IR gives well-defined absorption peaks with high selectivity for liquids, which helps reveal what differs in an unknown contaminated sample, and ATR fiber probes analyse liquids without sampling. [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.10; S01 p.9]
- **NIR (transmission/transflection)** (Compare): NIR is the most common online fiber-probe method and transmission/transflection suits liquids, but its broad overlapping bands need chemometrics and it may lack sensitivity below 0.1 %. [S01 p.10; S01 Table 1 (p.6); S01 p.16]
- **Raman** (Compare): Raman carries information complementary to MIR and suits aqueous media, but needs special probes and can be masked by fluorescence. [S01 p.5; S01 p.16; S01 p.11]
- **Fluorescence / UV-Vis** (Only if needed): Both are very sensitive but have low selectivity, and fluorescence only works if the contaminant fluoresces, so they are screened only to check whether the contaminant gives such a signal. [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## Multispectral option

**MIR ATR + Raman**: Raman carries information complementary to MIR, and combining heterogeneous information from two methods is the basis of multispectral synergy in complex media.. Probe: separate synchronised probes for the lab screening; a combined probe (same spot) only if the liquid proves heterogeneous or rapidly changing. Fusion: model per method first, then low-level fusion of synchronised spectra with a dedicated data-fusion algorithm. Prove the gain: The fused model must separate good and off-spec samples better than the best single method on the same validation data. [S01 p.5; S01 p.7; S01 p.8; S01 p.14; S01 p.9; S01 p.14–15]

## Clarification questions

1. Which quality variable goes off specification, and how is off-specification currently determined in your laboratory? (Method selection requires a defined analytical problem and an independent reference analysis to build and validate models.) [S01 p.5]
2. Can you supply retained good and off-specification samples of the liquid, together with any suspected contaminant candidates? (Comparing spectra of real samples with several methods is how the suitable method is selected for incompletely known compositions.) [S01 p.3; S02 p.3]
3. Is the liquid transparent, turbid or opaque, and is it aqueous or organic? (Optical density and medium decide between transmission/transflection, ATR or reflection probes and affect Raman suitability.) [S01 p.16; S01 p.4]
4. Where would the sensor be installed and what are the temperature, pressure and distance to a possible analyzer location? (Probe interface and fiber type (e.g. long silica cables or high-temperature coated fibers) depend on location and conditions.) [S02 p.7; S01 p.15]
5. How small a deviation must be detected, and how quickly must an off-spec event be flagged? (Sensitivity differs strongly between methods (e.g. NIR may lack sensitivity below 0.1 %), so acceptance criteria drive the choice.) [S01 p.16; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]

## First feasibility experiment

Find out which optical method, or combination, sees a reproducible spectral difference between good and off-specification liquid samples. [S05 p.1; S02 p.20; S01 p.3; S01 p.9]

1. Collect retained good and off-specification samples and have each analysed independently in the client's laboratory to define its quality status.
2. Measure all samples with the multi-spectral fiber system: MIR ATR, NIR transmission/transflection, Raman and fluorescence/UV-Vis.
3. Compare spectra by multivariate analysis to see which method separates good from off-spec samples and identify the key spectral features.
4. If two methods show complementary differences, fuse their data and check whether the combination beats the best single method on the same validation samples.
5. Report the recommended method and only then define probe, fiber and in-line location for a process trial.

References: independent laboratory analysis of each sample's quality status / contaminant (method to be named by the client)
Go / no-go: criteria come from the client
