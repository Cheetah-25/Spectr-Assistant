## Risks and problems with the request

- **Won't work:** The kidney study is not proof of surgical readiness: it tested two prototypes on 14 in vitro samples from 7 patients, used thawed frozen biopsies whose spectra can differ from in vivo, and the authors call it a proof of concept with very high variance in cancer tissue, unacceptable false negatives and much work remaining. [S03 p.1; S03 p.3; S03 p.14]
- **Wrong assumption:** The study may only be quoted as an in vitro proof of concept that an LED-based NIR sensor captured tumour-related variance in kidney samples; the surgical scenario in S01 is an application concept, not validation, and neither applies to a different sensor, organ or set-up without new data. [S03 p.1; S03 p.2; S01 p.13]
- **Check first:** The LED number and wavelengths must be adapted to each tissue and cancer type, so check whether your sensor matches the study prototypes and your target organ before reusing any of its results. [S03 p.14]
- **Check first:** With few patients, any model on your setup needs thorough validation such as segmented cross-validation and randomisation tests before its discrimination can be trusted. [S03 p.14; S03 p.9]

## Methods worth testing

- **NIR diffuse reflection (LED or full spectrum)** (Main): It is the method of the cited study, penetrates tissue up to about 3 mm and four simulated LED channels kept the diagnostic capability of full spectra in vitro. [S03 p.2; S03 p.10]
- **MIR ATR + fluorescence combined probe** (Compare): Combined fluorimetry and ATR IR has been reported to detect tumour boundaries and a single probe measures both at the same spot. [S04 p.4; S04 p.1; S01 p.7]
- **Fluorescence alone** (Only if needed): It is sensitive for biological fluorophores such as FAD but has low selectivity and alone did not separate all tissue classes. [S01 p.5; S04 p.3; S04 p.4; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]
- **Raman** (Only if needed): It carries information complementary to MIR but its weak signal is often masked by much stronger fluorescence of organic compounds. [S01 p.5; S01 p.11]

## Multispectral option

**MIR ATR + fluorescence (compared against NIR alone)**: MIR ATR sees molecular bands such as the ester carbonyl and amide bands while fluorescence sees intrinsic fluorophores such as porphyrin and FAD, and in model tissue neither alone separated all classes while the fused data did.. Probe: combined probe (same spot). Fusion: low-level fusion of synchronised spectra with a dedicated data-fusion algorithm. Prove the gain: The fused model must beat the best single method (NIR, MIR ATR or fluorescence) on the same patient-segmented validation data with fewer false negatives. [S04 p.4; S04 p.3; S01 p.8; S04 p.2; S01 p.9; S01 p.7]

## First feasibility experiment

Establish on your own setup whether NIR reflectance, alone or reduced to a few LED channels, separates histologically confirmed tumour from normal tissue ex vivo, and whether a combined MIR ATR + fluorescence probe does better. [S03 p.10; S03 p.14; S03 p.3; S04 p.2; S02 p.20]

1. Collect paired tumour and normal ex vivo samples from several patients, each with independent histology, and record how samples were stored and thawed.
2. Measure full NIR diffuse reflectance spectra at several points per sample on your own probe and spectrometer.
3. Simulate your sensor's LED channels from the full spectra by weighting with the real LED emission spectra and compare full-spectrum and reduced models.
4. Measure the same points with a combined MIR ATR + fluorescence probe and build single-method and fused models on the same data.
5. Validate all models with patient-segmented cross-validation and randomisation tests and report false negatives against your go/no-go criteria.

References: Independent histology of each sample
Go / no-go: Acceptable false-negative rate and specificity to be defined by the client; in vivo or surgical use requires separate clinical validation.

- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman needs special probes, is recorded in the visible/NIR region, and carries information complementary to MIR.
- **S01 p.11** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman signals are weak and often masked by laser scatter and, for many organic compounds, by much stronger fluorescence; special filters and probe designs are needed. The Raman shift (cm⁻¹) is the spectral axis.
- **S01 p.8** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): For heterogeneous samples the different methods must see the same small area; alternating two probes makes this hard, multispectral probes solve it. Data from methods with different acquisition rates must be synchronised.
- **S01 p.9** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fused data blocks differ in noise and information content; normalisation alone does not fix this, so dedicated data-fusion algorithms are needed.
