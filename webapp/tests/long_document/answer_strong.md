## Methods worth testing

- **NIR (transmission/transflection; diffuse reflection for MP-2)** (Main): NIR is the most common online fiber-probe method, works with silica fibers that can run 100 to 300 m to an analyzer house, and covers the moisture bands at 1.3 to 2.2 µm; its overlapping bands need a chemometric model. [S01 p.10; S01 p.5; S02 p.7; S02 p.18]
- **Raman (785 nm excitation)** (Compare): Raman carries information complementary to IR, AFS has measured Raman spectra of refined rapeseed oil (2600–3100 cm-1 shown), and it uses silica fibers; its signals are weak and can be masked by fluorescence of organic compounds, so this must be checked on the yellow ester phase. [S01 p.5; S02 p.16; S02 p.7; S01 p.11]
- **MIR ATR** (Compare): Mid-IR gives well-defined absorption peaks and AFS shows water-in-oil ATR spectra (4000–2000 cm-1); it can be screened on the client's existing lab FTIR with diamond ATR, but the thin ATR layer and the lack of long-fiber evidence limit it in-line. [S01 p.5; S02 p.13; Doc p.6; S02 p.9]
- **UV-Vis** (Not suitable): UV-Vis has low selectivity and strongly overlapping signals in real mixtures, which does not fit separate ester, methanol and water values. [S01 p.5; S01 Table 1 (p.6)]
- **Fluorescence** (Not suitable): Fluorimetry relies on emission of certain, mostly biological substances; most chemicals do not fluoresce, so it gives no direct route to ester, methanol or water. [S01 p.5; S01 p.12]

## Multispectral option

**NIR + Raman**: NIR sees hydrogen bonds and polar groups (water, methanol O-H), while Raman sees the molecular backbone and non-polar groups (ester chain), so the information is complementary; both run on silica fibers and can share the analyzer-house location.. Probe: Separate synchronised probes at the clear points MP-1 and MP-3; at the turbid MP-2 a combined probe seeing the same spot is preferable because heterogeneous samples need the same small area.. Fusion: Low-level fusion of synchronised spectra with PLS, using dedicated data-fusion algorithms because the blocks differ in noise and information content.. Prove the gain: Build NIR-only, Raman-only and fused models on the same calibration samples and compare them on the same independent validation set against the laboratory references; the combination is only kept if it beats the best single method. [S01 p.14–15; S01 p.15; S01 p.5; S02 p.7; S01 p.8; S01 p.9; S01 p.7]

- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): NIR penetrates up to several millimetres even into dense solids (low absorption, strong scattering), works with ordinary glass optics and standard lamps, which makes it suited to online use with remote fiber probes.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman needs special probes, is recorded in the visible/NIR region, and carries information complementary to MIR.
- **S01 p.11** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Raman signals are weak and often masked by laser scatter and, for many organic compounds, by much stronger fluorescence; special filters and probe designs are needed. The Raman shift (cm⁻¹) is the spectral axis.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Strong mid-IR absorption limits MIR to very thin layers (usually <50 µm), which is why MIR is used with ATR attachments and probes.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): UV-Vis is very sensitive, especially in the UV region (190–360 nm), but its strongly overlapping signals complicate the analysis of real mixtures, so chemometrics is required.
- **S01 p.5** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fluorimetry is highly sensitive but relies on secondary emission of certain substances, mostly of biological origin; most chemicals do not fluoresce.
- **S01 p.12** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fluorimetry measures broad emission from certain samples under UV/visible excitation; it is frequently used in biology and biotechnology and can be combined with MIR or NIR.
- **S01 p.15** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): The cited feed-frame study reports that fused NIR+Raman PLS models predicted API potency more accurately than single-method models (in that study).
- **S01 p.8** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): For heterogeneous samples the different methods must see the same small area; alternating two probes makes this hard, multispectral probes solve it. Data from methods with different acquisition rates must be synchronised.
- **S01 p.9** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Fused data blocks differ in noise and information content; normalisation alone does not fix this, so dedicated data-fusion algorithms are needed.
- **S01 p.7** (S01_Multispectral_Laboratory.pdf): Complex media (oil, milk, fermentation media, tissue) may need two or more methods; examples: NIR+fluorescence gave more accurate biomass in yeast fermentation and distinguished intra- and extracellular metabolites; NIR+IR showed benefits for tumour boundaries; fluorescence+IR was needed to classify tissues; NIR+Raman gave the best results for pellet coating.
